# Khanmigo's reality check: ambition meets engagement

**Khan Academy's Khanmigo, the AI tutor Sal Khan promised would deliver Benjamin Bloom's "2 sigma" learning miracle, has reached unprecedented scale but produced modest, narrowly distributed gains—and Khan himself now concedes it has been "a non-event" for most students.** Three years after launching as an OpenAI GPT-4 flagship partner in March 2023, Khanmigo has grown to roughly **2 million users across 795 U.S. districts and 180+ countries**, yet Khan Academy publicly admitted in May 2026 that only **15% of students with access regularly engage with the tutor**. The most cited efficacy result—a **+6-point gain on New Jersey's NJSLA math test** in Newark—was produced by Khan Academy's *practice platform*, not the AI tutor, and accrued only to the small minority of students who mastered 60+ skills annually. The case illustrates both the operational possibility of deploying conversational AI at K-12 scale and the persistent gap between AI's tutoring promise and its classroom reality.

## From GPT-4 launch partner to global teacher utility

Khanmigo debuted on **March 14, 2023**, alongside OpenAI's public release of GPT-4. Khan Academy had been quietly testing the model since September 2022, making it one of five named GPT-4 launch partners alongside Microsoft, Stripe, Duolingo, and Be My Eyes. Sal Khan's launch blog framed the moment in civilizational terms: *"AI is nascent technology, but we already find ourselves at a critical juncture. Will we amplify its benefits equally across society?"* A month later at TED 2023, Khan delivered "How AI Could Save (Not Destroy) Education," pledging to *"give every student on the planet an artificially intelligent but amazing personal tutor"* and explicitly invoking Bloom's 1984 finding that one-to-one tutoring produces gains of two standard deviations over conventional instruction.

The product itself comprises two surfaces. **Khanmigo for Teachers** generates lesson plans, rubrics, exit tickets, IEP drafts, leveled texts, parent newsletters, and report-card comments through a UI-driven interface requiring no prompt engineering; Khan Academy estimates it saves teachers roughly **five hours per week**. **Khanmigo for Students** provides Socratic 1:1 tutoring on Khan Academy exercises, a Writing Coach that tracks revisions and flags pasted content, math tutoring with image upload, debate practice, coding feedback, and historical-character roleplay. The signature design choice is "tutor not solver": when a student gives a wrong answer, an internal AI "thought" instructs the model not to reveal the mistake but to ask the student to explain their reasoning. Common Sense Media rated it **four stars** in November 2023, above ChatGPT and Bard.

Pricing has fallen aggressively. Original individual access cost **$9/month or $99/year** as a "recurring donation" to offset GPT-4 API costs; district pricing began at **$60/student/year**. By November 2023, individual access dropped to **$4/month** and districts to $35; by 2024-25, districts paid just **$15/student/year**. The pivotal commercial event was the **May 21, 2024 Microsoft partnership** announced at Microsoft Build: Microsoft donated Azure AI-optimized infrastructure, migrated Khanmigo from OpenAI's direct API to **Azure OpenAI Service**, and effectively underwrote free Khanmigo for Teachers access for all U.S. educators. By August 2024, free teacher access extended to **180+ countries**. Microsoft CTO Kevin Scott framed Khan Academy's value as reputational: *"They just have a very long reputation now of very successfully delivering these educational materials to learners everywhere in the world."*

## Scale grew explosively, but engagement lagged

Khan Academy's official **SY24-25 Annual Report** records roughly **2.0 million global Khanmigo users**, a **731% year-over-year jump**. Within that, roughly **770,000 U.S. K-12 students** used Khanmigo through district partnerships across **795 districts**, while nearly **one million grassroots teachers** used the free Microsoft-subsidized version across 70+ countries. Chief Learning Officer Kristen DiCerbo told K-12 Dive in August 2025 that the K-12 student count jumped from **40,000 in SY23-24 to 700,000 in SY24-25**, calling it *"the biggest one-year jump that I have seen in terms of adoption of an education technology."* By April 2025, Khan reported on the *Unsupervised Learning* podcast that Khanmigo had reached **1.4 million users**, far exceeding his original 2025 target of 100,000.

State and large-district deployments anchor the footprint. **New Hampshire** became the first state-level partner in June 2024 and renewed through SY25-26, covering 50 districts, ~5,000 educators, and ~40,000 students. **Rhode Island** signed statewide in December 2024. **Indiana** awarded ~$2 million in pandemic-relief grants under its AI-Powered Platform Pilot, and **20 of 36 grant-recipient districts (56%) chose Khanmigo** over competitors like Amira, Edia, and SchoolJoy. **Palm Beach County, Florida** (~183,000 students) rolled Khanmigo to all middle and high schools in 2024-25, backed by a **$2 million Stiles-Nicholson Foundation grant**. Internationally, Khan Academy's Philippines operation scaled from 34 schools to **1,500+ serving 600,000 students**, and India's **Karnataka** became the first state-level partner in Asia.

Yet the headline scale figure masked a quieter story underneath. On **May 5, 2026**, Khan Academy publicly disclosed that despite **108 million total Khanmigo interactions since 2023** and roughly **269,000 weekday interactions on average**, **only about 15% of students with access regularly engage with the tutor**. That admission, paired with Sal Khan's April 2026 comments, triggered the most significant product redesign of Khanmigo's history.

## Newark's "+6 points" — the headline number, contextualized

The most-cited evidence for Khanmigo's effectiveness is a longitudinal panel study from **Newark Public Schools**, New Jersey's largest district. **Superintendent Roger León** began the partnership in 2022-23 as a math-curriculum supplement, expanded it to all 13 North Ward K-8 schools by 2023-24, and signed a formal data-sharing agreement in March 2024. Bill Gates visited First Avenue Elementary in May 2024 and his foundation funded a $25,000 license expansion grant. By 2025-26 Khan Academy described its reach as **"29,000 students across 66 schools."**

The technical evaluation—**Yamkovenko & Grimaldi (2025)**, posted on Khan Academy's blog in August and republished November 21, 2025—used a two-way fixed-effects panel regression on roughly **5,200 students across 13 North Ward schools** (not 29,000 across 66 schools, as the marketing materials might imply). The headline finding is that each additional hour of Khan Academy practice raised NJSLA math scores by **+0.006 SD**, and each additional skill mastered by **+0.003 SD**. Translated: **2-4 NJSLA points** for adding 30 minutes/week of practice, and **5-8 points** for mastering 2 additional skills/week. Students who became "Yearly Proficient Learners" by mastering 60+ skills hit the upper bound, **gaining +6 points versus the New Jersey average of +2—triple the state's typical growth.** The authors note gains were *"significantly larger for students who began below the proficiency cut-score."*

Three caveats matter. First, the study evaluates **Khan Academy's practice platform**, not Khanmigo specifically; Khan Academy itself acknowledged in late 2024 that *"Khanmigo-focused efficacy studies… are underway."* Second, it was authored by Khan Academy's own efficacy staff and is not peer-reviewed. Third, Newark's overall math proficiency remains far below state averages: **21% in 2025 versus 41% statewide**, with Chalkbeat noting *"it's unclear how much of Khanmigo's tutoring in classrooms impacted that rate."* León framed the work as foundational rather than transformative, telling his board in March 2024: *"The strategy here is not to do it in any one school only. This is a program that we are piloting and then it will flourish."* Sal Khan added in *School Administrator*: *"In Newark, over 70 percent of students are engaging with Khan Academy at a level we know correlates with real academic gain—30 minutes per week. I suspect Khanmigo is reducing the number of Newark students in credit recovery."*

The strongest peer-reviewed evidence is **Eames, Brunskill, Yamkovenko, Weatherholtz, & Oreopoulos (2026)** in *PNAS* (Jan 6, 2026)—a Stanford/University of Toronto/Khan Academy collaboration analyzing ~200,000 students across hundreds of districts using Khan Academy's MAP Accelerator. It found a **+0.031 SD math gain per 6.6 hours of annual Khan Academy practice (~11 min/week)**. But the same study found *"lower-performing students, though, saw few if any improvements from Khan Academy"*—and again, this evaluated the practice platform, not the AI tutor. **No rigorous third-party RCT of Khanmigo itself has been published as of May 2026.**

## Hobart Indiana: the 60 Minutes story and what came after

The 4,000-student **School City of Hobart** in northwest Indiana became Khanmigo's other flagship case study. Superintendent **Peggy Buffington** had partnered with Khan Academy since 2017 and was one of the first cohort to pilot Khanmigo in spring 2023. The district was prominently featured on **CBS's 60 Minutes on December 8, 2024**, with Anderson Cooper highlighting safety features Buffington helped design: *"One of the pieces of feedback that we gave to Sal was just that safety net. 'What if there are students out there that are struggling with some emotional things? Maybe suicide ideation?'"* Chemistry teacher Melissa Higgason described Khanmigo as triage for her 30-student classes: *"If I can't get to all 30 of my students and someone's stuck and can't move forward, it's a place that they can go and they can ask a quick question."* Hobart used Indiana's grant program to subsidize the $15-per-student cost.

But Chalkbeat's April 2026 follow-up revealed a more complicated picture. Hobart High School geometry teacher **Kristen Musall**—herself an early adopter—**has stopped using Khanmigo entirely** in her classroom. Her assessment: *"If students don't engage with the material enough to know what they're looking for, then an AI like Khanmigo doesn't necessarily help."* She also reported *"more enthusiasm for the product among administrators than teachers in her school."* Buffington remained measured: *"It's like anything in education. You have to learn how to use the tool and use it appropriately."* No quantitative outcomes have been published for Hobart specifically.

## Sal Khan's striking concession

The most consequential commentary on Khanmigo's impact has come from Khan himself. In a **Chalkbeat interview published April 9, 2026** by Matt Barnum—titled *"Why Sal Khan's AI revolution hasn't happened yet, according to Sal Khan"*—Khan delivered the line that has come to define the product's reckoning:

> **"For a lot of students, it was a non-event. They just didn't use it much."**

> "Imagine he walked into a class, sat in the back of the room, and waited for students to seek out help. Some will; most won't."

> "I just view it as part of the solution; I don't view it as the end-all and be-all. AI is going to help, but I think our biggest lever is really investing in the human systems."

DiCerbo, the Chief Learning Officer, was equally candid: **"Students aren't great at asking questions well… So far I am not seeing the revolution in education."** A Khan Academy spokesperson explained the product overhaul as direct response: *"Students were not seeking out Khanmigo's help as much as we had hoped."*

The rhetorical retreat is dramatic when set against the 2023 TED talk. Khan had predicted Khanmigo would cut **"90% of teachers' admin tasks"** by end of 2024 (a deadline he later extended to 2034). The "biggest lever is the human systems" framing essentially inverts the original AI-tutor-for-every-student thesis. MIT's **Justin Reich** captured the implication for the broader sector: *"Venture-adjacent friends: your pope took off his hat… What kind of thesis does venture-funded ed-tech have if the biggest lever for change is human systems?"*

## What's not working: hallucinations, disengagement, and the 5 Percent Problem

Three structural problems have dogged the product. First, **math hallucinations are persistent and well-documented**. The *Wall Street Journal* found Khanmigo praising incorrect arithmetic with *"Excellent!"* on a right-triangle problem (accepting 430 instead of 440 for 27²−17²). CNN's Nadia Bidarian reported in August 2023: *"Khanmigo told me that my answer to 10,332 divided by 4 was incorrect three times before correcting me by sending me the same number."* Hechinger's Jill Barshay independently reproduced errors on algebra problems. NYU computer scientist Ernest Davis explained the root cause: *"Just looking at a lot of texts and trying to figure out the patterns that constitute multiplication is not a very effective way of getting to a computer program that can do multiplication reliably."* Berkeley researchers Pardos and Bhandari found out-of-box GPT made errors on **47% of intermediate algebra problems**, though mitigation techniques could shrink this dramatically.

Second, **engagement has been the dominant failure mode**. Khan Academy's own pre-Khanmigo research found only **11% of students used the platform at the recommended 30-minutes/week dosage**, and Laurence Holt's "5 Percent Problem" analysis showed Khan's strongest effect sizes (0.26 SD) were achieved only after excluding 95% of the study population. The new Khanmigo numbers—15% regular engagement—reproduce this familiar curve. Critics argue the Socratic design itself is part of the problem: Stanford's **Dan Meyer** documented that Khanmigo treats thoughtful partial answers identically to gibberish, and **Ben Riley** of Cognitive Resonance showed in *The 74* that the tutor can lead students down *"an error-filled path of miscalculation, misunderstanding and wasted effort."* An independent peer-reviewed comparison in the *Journal of Teaching and Learning* (2025) found **no statistically significant differences in learning outcomes between students using Khanmigo and students using Google search**.

Third, the **product has been rebuilt to compensate**. In October 2025 Khan Academy announced "Reimagined Khan Academy Districts," with full rollout for back-to-school 2026. Khanmigo shifts from a sidebar chat destination to an **embedded coach woven into practice flows**—proactive rather than reactive, appearing during problem-solving and prompting prerequisite review whether or not the student asks. Lead engineer Shawn Jansepar acknowledged the previous design's failure: *"Some kids found it boring and a bit confusing, and teachers lacked visibility about district expectations. So we started from scratch."* Audrey Watters' acerbic read of the pivot: *"Don't want it? Too bad. It's part of the curricular infrastructure now, suckers."* Khan Academy is now tracking a new primary metric—**"next-item correctness,"** measuring independent problem-solving *after* AI assistance—an implicit admission that interaction volume alone meant little.

## Funding architecture and 2026 strategic pivots

Khanmigo's economics depend heavily on **subsidy stacking**. Microsoft Azure underwrites the free global teacher tier; the Gates Foundation, Chan Zuckerberg Initiative (which committed **$6 million over three years to evaluate Khanmigo**), the **Amgen Foundation** ($10M+ cumulative, funding science content), the Stiles-Nicholson Foundation, Overdeck, Doerr, Siegel, Yass, Carnegie, Lemann, and Rockefeller all appear in Khan Academy's SY24-25 donor list. State and district fees (Indiana's $50,000 grants, Newark's Gates-funded licenses, Palm Beach's $2M foundation grant) round out implementation costs. Khan Academy's SY24-25 financials showed **$128M revenue against $95M expenses**; no major layoffs have been publicly reported.

The most consequential 2025-26 developments suggest a pivot beyond the original AI-tutor thesis:

- **January 21, 2026 (BETT London)**: Google announced that **Gemini will power Khan Academy's Writing Coach and forthcoming Reading Coach**, ending OpenAI/Microsoft exclusivity and diversifying the model stack. The Writing Coach is now free for all U.S. teachers via Microsoft sponsorship, and Khan Academy content is being embedded directly inside Gemini for Indian audiences (CBSE/NCERT-aligned, including Hindi and "Hinglish").
- **April 14, 2026 (TED2026 Vancouver)**: Khan Academy, TED, and ETS announced the **Khan TED Institute**—a competency-based, AI-focused bachelor's degree under $10,000, with corporate partners Google, Microsoft, Accenture, Bain, McKinsey, and Replit. Applications expected in 2027. This is a notable move beyond K-12 toward higher-ed credentialing.
- **March-April 2026 features**: New motivation tools (team games, classroom leaderboards), an **"Explain Your Thinking"** assessment that mimics teacher-student reasoning conversations, image upload for handwritten math, and Khanmigo availability in Arabic, Chinese, Russian, Ukrainian, Urdu, and Vietnamese.

## The 2 Sigma gap and what it reveals

Set Khan's TED promise against the evidence and the gap is wide. Bloom's 1984 finding was that 1:1 tutoring plus mastery learning produced gains of roughly **two standard deviations**—the average tutored student outperforming 98% of conventionally taught peers. The strongest Khanmigo-adjacent evidence (the PNAS 2026 study) shows **+0.031 SD per 6.6 hours of annual practice**, and the Newark white paper's outer-bound +6 NJSLA points represents perhaps 0.15-0.20 SD even for the small minority of students hitting the 60+ skills threshold. Stanford's SCALE Initiative concluded in March 2026 that the AI-in-education evidence base remains *"extremely limited."*

What Khanmigo has demonstrated, more modestly but still meaningfully, is operational. **Khan Academy has put a conversational AI in front of roughly 2 million users across 795 districts at $15/student or free**, with reasonable safety guardrails, integrated rostering, district-grade compliance, and measurable—if uneven—productivity gains for teachers. Survey data from Indiana's pilot showed **53% of teachers rated platform impact on students "positive" or "very positive"** and identified lesson-planning time savings as the dominant benefit. That is a real distribution achievement, even if it is not the educational revolution Khan promised.

## Conclusion: ambition recalibrated, infrastructure built

Three years in, Khanmigo's story is less a refutation of AI tutoring than a clarification of what the technology actually delivers at K-12 scale. The infrastructure works: 108 million interactions, 795 districts, free global teacher access via Microsoft, and multi-model architecture incorporating both GPT-4o and Gemini. The pedagogy works in narrow conditions: students who voluntarily engage at recommended dosage levels post real but modest gains, with somewhat larger benefits for those starting below proficiency. The revolution, however, has not arrived—because **most students with access don't use the tutor, the small share who do are disproportionately the already-engaged, and the underlying language model still makes math errors that erode trust**. Khan's own retreat—"AI is going to help, but I think our biggest lever is really investing in the human systems"—may be the most important sentence yet spoken by an AI-in-education leader, and it should reshape how districts evaluate the next wave of AI tutoring claims. The Khanmigo experiment has produced a working delivery system in search of a learning effect that, for now, lives mostly in the upper decile of students willing to seek it out.
