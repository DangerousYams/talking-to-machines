# Five Steps to Raise AIA's Marketing Bar With AI

## Frame to set first

Cultivated AI doesn't run AIA's LinkedIn. We build the AI muscle into the team. We ship the first ten things side by side, then the team owns it. The reason this works for AIA specifically is the moat: 40 years of mine-by-mine wear records that no competitor has. We turn that into a publishing engine competitors cannot copy.

---

## Step 1: Diagnose. (Weeks 1-2)
Run the AI Shelf Audit and a parallel ICP workshop.

**The audit.** 50 buyer-persona prompts run through Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini. Score every answer on three axes: was AIA mentioned, was it specific, was it accurate. Compare side by side to Magotteaux, Molycop, Weir, FLSmidth.

**The ICP workshop.** Half a day with AIA's sales leadership. Define Tier 1 (top 50 named mining and cement accounts where AIA must win), Tier 2 (the next 200), Tier 3 (the long tail). For each Tier 1 account, name the buying committee: mill superintendent, comminution engineer, COO, procurement head.

**Output.** A baseline showing AIA's "AI shelf presence" today, plus a target list of 50 accounts and ~250 named buyers the content will be pointed at.

**Watch out for.** Sales reps will argue "everyone is a customer." Force the choice. Without a Tier 1 list, the content has no edge.

---

## Step 2: Excavate. (Weeks 2-4)
Mine AIA's own proprietary data into a publishable corpus.

This is the step most consultants skip and the one that makes the difference. AIA has 40 years of mine-by-mine wear records, hundreds of application engineering trial reports, and metallurgical case data that nobody else owns. Today it sits in PDFs and engineers' heads.

Sit with R&D, application engineering, plant heads. Pull the material out. Sanitize it. Strip customer names where confidentiality requires. Get legal sign-off on what can be published. Structure it so an AI agent can use it: indexed, tagged by ore type, mill type, geography, alloy grade.

**Output.** A clean internal RAG corpus that becomes the fuel for every piece of content. Without this, AI gives you generic copy that sounds like everyone else's. With it, AI produces content only AIA could publish.

**Watch out for.** The technical team will be precious about the data. Frame this as "we are not giving the data to anyone, we are building YOUR content engine." Use Claude Enterprise or Azure OpenAI with no-training contracts.

---

## Step 3: Capture. (Weeks 3-4, parallel to Step 2)
Record the human voices.

The single biggest reason AI marketing content fails in technical sales is that it sounds like AI. The fix is to capture real human voice profiles before any content gets written.

Three interviews:
- **Bhadresh Shah, 90 minutes.** Views on India manufacturing, hi-chrome vs forged, where the grinding industry is going, what he is proud of, what he refuses to do. Let him talk.
- **Chief Metallurgist or R&D head, 60 minutes.** The deep technical voice. Specific opinions on alloy chemistry, heat treatment, mill liner design.
- **One Vega regional head (LATAM or CIS), 60 minutes.** The customer-facing voice. Stories from mines.

Transcribe each. Build a voice profile in Claude for each: cadence, idioms, unfashionable opinions, the words they never use.

**Output.** Three voice profiles that produce drafts in each person's actual voice. The person edits. They do not write.

**Watch out for.** Bhadresh might resist publishing under his name. Negotiate a starting cadence of one post per fortnight, ghosted but personally edited. He gets veto on every word. Build trust before scaling.

---

## Step 4: Build. (Weeks 4-7)
Stand up the content engine and ship the first eight weeks.

- **LinkedIn cadence.** 3 posts/week from the corporate page, 1/week from each named expert, 1/fortnight from Bhadresh. Six to eight posts per week total.
- **First five case studies**, structured in the "named mine + named challenge + a real number + named AIA expert" format that Weir and Molycop use. Drafted by AI from the corpus, edited by the technical team, customer-cleared, published as LinkedIn document carousels and gated PDFs on the website.
- **The account briefing agent.** A Claude project loaded with the corpus plus public data on the top 50 accounts. Sales reps query it before every Tier 1 meeting and get a one-page brief: recent customer news, buying committee, talking points, relevant AIA case data.
- **Employee advocacy.** Onboard 15 field engineers across LATAM, India, CIS, Africa onto a low-cost tool (DSMN8 or GaggleAMP). They reshare corporate posts with their own commentary in their region's language.
- **Regional language layer.** AI-translate the highest-engagement English posts into Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, Arabic. Vega's regional reps publish them.

**Output.** A working publishing system shipping 6-8 posts/week, 5 published case studies, an active sales briefing agent, and 15 amplifying field engineers in 4 languages.

**Watch out for.** Don't ship anything technical without the SME signing off. One bad alloy claim destroys the program's credibility.

---

## Step 5: Transfer. (Weeks 7-8 plus ongoing)
Hand over the keys to AIA's team.

The whole point is AIA's team runs this after we leave. Otherwise we have built a dependency, not a capability.

- Document the operating playbook: weekly content calendar, prompts, voice profiles, case-study template, legal review checklist, publishing schedule.
- Train the AIA marketing team (and the SMEs) on the AI tooling. Hands-on. Not a slide deck.
- Set up a monthly office-hours retainer for 6 months: one call a month, troubleshoot, raise the bar.
- Quarterly AI Shelf re-audit to measure: are the buyer prompts now returning AIA? Is the answer specific? Is it accurate? This becomes the scoreboard.

**Output.** AIA's team owns the engine. We shift from build mode to coaching mode. Success metric: the AI Shelf Audit moves from "AIA gets a line" to "AIA gets a paragraph with named experts and named numbers."

**Watch out for.** Without the office-hours retainer, the system decays in 90 days. Build the retainer in from day one.
