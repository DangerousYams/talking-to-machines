# The ground beneath AIA Engineering is moving

**AIA Engineering's public AI footprint is effectively zero — and that silence is itself the pitch.** Magotteaux has publicly committed machine-learning models to predict grinding-media alloy performance. Moly-Cop has put sensors *inside* SAG balls and built a digital twin platform. Weir bought an AI computer-vision company and a Swedish AI process-optimization startup. Meanwhile, every major AIA customer — BHP, Newmont, Rio Tinto, Vale, Freeport, Codelco, Anglo American, Tata Steel, JSW, Hindustan Zinc, UltraTech, Adani Cement — has either deployed or named-and-funded an AI program with quantified outcomes. The strategic encirclement is unambiguous: mining-equipment OEMs and wear-parts competitors are bundling AI directly with their hardware to predict, and sell, wear life. AIA's 40+ years of proprietary mine-by-mine wear data is arguably the most valuable, undeveloped AI asset in the global wear-parts industry — and the window to build a moat from it is narrowing because **62% of mining AI investment now happens through M&A** rather than internal R&D (Mind the Bridge × BHP, October 2025). This brief equips you for a conversation that lands two truths: the ground is genuinely moving in their industry, and the leaders winning are doing it by thinking clearly about their own data, not by buying tools.

## The five stories to drop in the room tomorrow

These are the highest-density, citable, recent examples — pick the two or three that match Bhadresh Shah's mood in the moment.

**1. Newmont Cadia: $6.5 million a year from an HPGR/SAG controller.** At Cadia in New South Wales — a mine AIA almost certainly supplies — Newmont transferred a Boddington-developed ML controller onto the HPGR feeding the SAG mill and lifted throughput **80 tonnes/hour, equivalent to 600,000+ tonnes/year of additional mill feed**. Tom Palmer disclosed the number on the Q2 2024 earnings call; the Cadia Site Tour deck on 14 November 2024 attached the **$6.5M/yr** uplift figure. The "Full Potential" program contributed an estimated $200M of incremental value across the portfolio in 2025. The relevance: every extra tonne through that mill consumes more grinding media. AIA's customers are using AI to push throughput — that is constructive for media demand if AIA shows up with the analytics layer, and a margin squeeze if it does not.

**2. BHP Escondida: $18.9M uplift, three gigalitres of water saved.** BHP and Microsoft built an Azure ML system that makes hourly recommendations to operators of the Escondida concentrator. Mike Henry quoted the headline outcomes on BHP Insights (August 2024): **"more than three gigalitres of water — as well as 118-gigawatt hours of energy, since FY22."** Escondida produces over 1 Mt of copper a year; it is the largest copper mine on Earth. In May 2025, BHP launched a dedicated Industry AI Hub in Singapore — the first in mining. The buyer-education work for "AI = quantifiable ROI" has been done. The next conversation about a smart grinding-media offering will start with mining executives already convinced.

**3. Freeport-McMoRan Bagdad: an extra concentrator without the $1.5–2 billion capex.** McKinsey-built ML, ingesting sensor data from crushers, mills, and flotation cells, discovered Bagdad's ore wasn't one type but seven — and the AI prescribes a recipe per cluster. Outcomes (McKinsey case study, scaled across Americas operations): **+90,000 t/yr copper, ~$350–500M EBITDA at $4/lb, equivalent to bringing a new concentrator online without spending $1.5–2B or waiting 8–10 years**. The original Bagdad pilot avoided most of a planned $200M concentrator expansion. The lesson AIA's owner will recognize: the AI didn't replace metallurgy; it discovered structure in the metallurgy that humans had missed.

**4. Moly-Cop has put sensors inside grinding balls — and acquired three AI companies in 24 months.** Through the November 2020 acquisition of MILL Trakka, Moly-Cop integrated wireless sensors into its **SAG ball product itself**, delivering real-time data from inside the mill. Their "Molycop Digital Engine" — an ML/digital-twin platform for SAG mills — was presented at the SAG 2023 Vancouver conference. Quantified published claims on their MillSlicer VIP stack: **10–30% liner-life improvement, 5–10% grinding-media reduction, 3–40% throughput improvement**. Edikan Gold Mine in Ghana: **27% sustained throughput uplift in eight months**. Between 2020 and 2022 Moly-Cop acquired MILL Trakka, Digital Control Lab (MillSlicer), and Perth-based Process IQ; stood up "Molycop Digital" as a division under President Darren O'Connell. The world's other grinding-media incumbent is acquiring its way into the data layer that sits adjacent to its hardware.

**5. Magotteaux's quiet 2024 declaration.** Magotteaux's 2024 Integrated Annual Report contains a sentence Bhadresh Shah has almost certainly not read: *"Building on more than 40 years of data, Magotteaux developed **machine learning models to predict grinding media alloys performance**, helping customers optimize both wear and downstream processing."* The same report announces a group-wide MES rollout starting 2025 in Thailand and Mexico; a 2024 global partnership with **Stone Three**, a South African AI-comminution firm, to co-promote a "Comminution Booster"; and a five-year strategic partnership with **Codelco**, the world's largest copper miner. Add MagoSense (the KIMA Process Control-developed mill-shell monitor, presented at MEI Comminution '23 and pilot-validated at a South African concentrator) and the picture is unambiguous: Magotteaux is not announcing aspirations — it is executing.

## Why this is uniquely an opportunity for a wear-parts company

The reflex is to assume AI is for the OEMs and the miners. The deeper truth, well-supported by current research, is that a high-chrome casting company sits on a **uniquely defensible data asset** that competitors cannot replicate. Four specific frontiers matter.

**Smart grinding media and predictive wear.** Academic work has reached the point where XGBoost models predict SAG mill liner wear with **MAPE of around 5%** (MDPI Minerals 14:1200, 2024), and the same papers explicitly note that adding liner and ball material properties would close the residual gap. AIA owns precisely that proprietary data across more than a hundred unique mine, ore, and media combinations. The ML community is one published paper away from a generic open model; AIA can pre-empt it with a proprietary one whose moat is the data, not the algorithm.

**Generative alloy R&D.** Microsoft released **MatterGen** open-source in January 2025 — a diffusion model that designs inorganic crystals conditioned on target properties (bulk modulus, magnetic density, chemical system). Materials produced are more than twice as likely to be novel and stable, and over fifteen times closer to the local energy minimum, versus prior approaches. Google DeepMind's GNoME, paired with Berkeley's autonomous A-Lab, produced 41 new compounds in 17 days of independent operation. Academic reviews now identify ML and computational thermodynamics as the explicit future of high-chromium white cast iron alloy design (MDPI Metals 13:1831, 2023) — a space that has not yet seen commercial ML-driven optimization. Combined with **MAGMASOFT Autonomous Engineering** for casting parameters and CALPHAD thermodynamics, AIA has a credible 12-month path to a generative pipeline that produces ore-specific alloy candidates competitors cannot match without the underlying performance data.

**Casting-line computer vision.** YOLO-family defect detection and Fraunhofer-style 3D anomaly models on CT scans are now standard. PhysicsX reports models running over 100x faster than legacy methods deployed live on casting lines for real-time feedback. This is the most operationally mature, lowest-execution-risk AI application AIA could pilot inside its own foundries — a credibility-builder before the customer-facing work.

**AI-augmented customer engineering.** Caterpillar's **Cat Helios** platform — backed by 16 petabytes of data across 1.6 million connected assets — and the **Cat AI Assistant** launched at CES 2026 show the OEM customer-portal direction of travel. B2B aftermarket benchmarks are unambiguous: 73% of B2B buyers prefer self-service (Gartner 2026); AI-handled interaction costs $1–2 versus $15–25 for rep-assisted; manufacturers report 78% of orders moving to self-serve within five months. For AIA, the analog is a customer portal that predicts wear, recommends replacement timing, and quotes — converting field service from cost center to data-collection engine.
